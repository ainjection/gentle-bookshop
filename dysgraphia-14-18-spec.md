won't be seen using." The 14-18 book sells against a deadline. A 16-year-old with
dysgraphia does not need to relearn the alphabet. They need to get legible words down
fast enough to keep up in class and finish the paper.

## Listing description — paste as continuous paragraphs, no hard line breaks

By fifteen, nobody needs another workbook that starts with the letter A.

If your teenager has dysgraphia, the problem at this age is almost never that they cannot
form letters. It is that writing costs them so much effort they cannot keep up. Notes go
half-finished. Exam answers run out of time before they run out of ideas. The handwriting
falls apart in the last paragraph, exactly where the marks are.

This workbook is built for that, and only that. It is for ages 14 to 18, and it treats an
older student like an older student.

It starts by measuring. A timed baseline paragraph and a six-point legibility check, dated,
so there is something real to compare against later. Then the work: speed drills on the
words and joins that come up most, letter repair on only the letters that genuinely break
down under pressure, semi-cursive joins that buy speed without costing readability, real
note-taking from dictation at rising pace, and finally timed writing ruled to exam-paper
spacing. It ends where it began, with the same paragraph and the same timer, side by side
with page one.

WHAT IS INSIDE
- Timed baseline test and a dated legibility checklist
- Speed drills on high-frequency words and joins
- Letter repair for the pairs that actually fail: a/o, r/n, e/i, m/n, g/q, b/d
- Semi-cursive joins, and which ones to drop
- Abbreviation and margin systems for real note-taking
- Writing from dictation at increasing pace
- Timed paragraphs and full essay answers on exam-width ruling
- A closing re-test against the opening page

MADE FOR OLDER STUDENTS
- Left-handed friendly: model letters sit at both ends of every practice row, so a left
  hand never covers the example
- Three-zone guides with a dashed midline and a shaded zone for descenders
- One skill per page, ten focused minutes at a time
- Clean, grown-up design with no cartoons
- Large 8.5 x 11 pages with room to write

GREAT FOR high school and sixth-form students with dysgraphia, teenagers whose handwriting
collapses under time pressure, occupational therapy home practice, homeschool and special
education support, and anyone preparing for handwritten exams.

Note: this book measures progress rather than promising it. The re-test at the end is there
so you can see for yourself whether it worked.

## Page plan — 120 pages

The point of difference is that this book **measures**. Nothing else in the niche does.

1. **Baseline test (2pp).** Copy a fixed paragraph under a 3-minute timer. Count words
   written, tick a 6-point legibility checklist. Date it.
2. **Speed drills (24pp).** Timed repetitions of the highest-frequency words and joins.
   The actual high-school problem: keeping up with a talking teacher.
3. **Letter repair (20pp).** Only the letters that genuinely fail under speed — a/o, r/n,
   e/i, m/n, g/q, b/d, and the ascender/descender collisions. Not all 26. A 16-year-old
   does not need a page on the letter S.
4. **Joins and shortcuts (18pp).** Semi-cursive: which joins buy speed, which cost
   legibility, which to drop entirely.
5. **Real note-taking (24pp).** Abbreviation systems, headings, margin cues, and writing
   from dictation at increasing pace.
6. **Exam conditions (20pp).** Timed paragraphs and full essay answers, ruled to real
   exam-paper spacing.
7. **Re-test (2pp).** The same paragraph, same timer, same checklist. Side by side with
   page one.
8. Progress tracker and certificate (2pp+).

Carry over from the Teens book, they are already proven: trace-and-fade release, model
letters at both ends of every row, three-zone guides with dashed midline and shaded
descender zone, one skill per page, 8.5 x 11.

## Why the re-test matters commercially

Both existing books have zero reviews. A measurable before-and-after is the single
strongest trigger for a parent to come back and leave one, because it hands them proof
their money worked. It is also the honest claim: we are not promising neat handwriting,
we are handing them a way to see whether it changed.
